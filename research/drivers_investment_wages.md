# Driver tree: business investment and wages (agent output, 2026-10-26)
Caveat on our "capital deepening ≈72% of the market-sector slowdown": that is growth accounting (ABS 5260, reproduces PC); RBA (Feb 2026) argues shallow capital is partly a symptom of weak MFP/demand, not an independent cause. Treasury (ERR Aug 2025): capital deepening ≈ half of productivity growth since Federation.
Baseline (ABS 5206 Jun-2026): private business investment 17.1% of GDP (2012-13) → 11.0% (2020-21) → 12.1% (2025-26). Wage share of factor income 53.6% (2014-15) → 50.3% (2021-22) → 54.1% (2025-26).

## 1. Investment
(a) Hurdle rates [H fact/M weight]: firms require 12–15%+; NAB survey mean ≈13%; WACC ≈6% (−2pp since 2014); 3–5-yr payback rules common (RBA Dec 2021; Lane & Rosewall 2015). PC 2024 staff paper: risk premium +≈2pp post-GFC, no market-power cause found.
(b) Mining cycle [H]: mining investment ≈9% of GDP peak 2012-13; 2025-26 capex $54.8b mining vs $155.2b non-mining; most of the 17→11% fall is this unwind (mechanical); non-mining investment separately −3.0pp of GDP since GFC (PC Dec 2025).
(c) Competition [M]: markups +≈5% since mid-2000s (+12% digital-intensive) (Hambur 2021; Treasury 2023); top-4 share of sales 41%→43%; firm entry ≈13%→≈11% (e61); investment fell most among productive firms and in rising-markup sectors (RDP 2023-03); cost 1–3% of GDP (RDP 2025-05).
(d) Tax [H facts/M effect]: 30% statutory and effective above OECD avg; under imputation a cut benefits foreign investors first; PC proposes 20%/28% + 5% cash-flow tax, modelled +2.2% investment; instant write-offs (2010s, COVID) mostly did not lift investment (RDP 2025-01); 2026 Budget did not adopt cash-flow tax; made $20k write-off and carry-back permanent.
(e) Energy/uncertainty [L]: gas <2% of costs even for most gas-intensive manufacturers (RBA 2021); 2026 delay evidence only Ai Group advocacy survey (~220 firms).
(f) Approvals [M delay/L aggregate]: clean-energy approvals >500 days (PC); EPBC reform passed 28 Nov 2025 with 30-business-day pathway, untested.
(g) Housing credit [H fact/L cause]: housing share of non-financial credit 53.8% (2008) → 65.1% (2021) → 61.8% (Aug 2026); business 37.5% → 30.1% → 34.2% (RBA D2).
(h) Super [L]: ≈50% offshore (from ≈35%) (ASFA 2025); weak causal link (marginal investor is foreign, PC).
(i) Skills [L–M]: 29% of occupations in shortage 2025 (36% in 2023) (JSA).
(j) Intangibles [L]: ≈80% not capitalised (PC 2009); doesn't explain shallowing.
(k) Firm size [M]: top 20% of firms ≈80% of investment; ≈25% of small vs ≈80% of large firms invest in a quarter (RBA 2021).
(l) Data centres [H fact/L productivity]: telecoms/IT capex +76.8% y/y (Jun-q 2026); first 2026-27 capex estimate $200.7b; RBA: investment elevated "mainly" due to data centres; narrow (imported equipment, few jobs).

## 2. Wages
(a) Productivity [H]: real earnings and productivity each ≈1% p.a. since inflation targeting; WPI captures about half of trend productivity (Ross, RBA 22 Sep 2026); productivity ≈ 2019 level.
(b) Labour share [H]: back at 54.1%; 2023–26 real wage gains came from margins, channel nearly exhausted.
(c) Bargaining/minimum wages [M]: award-reliant ≈20% of employees, ≈10% of wage bill (RBA 2024); 2012–16 slowdown hit every pay-setting method → macro causes (RBA 2019); SJBP review: "early signs" of more bargaining, no quantified effect (DEWR Aug 2025); wage reviews: 2022 5.2%/4.6%; 2023 8.6%/5.75%; 2024 3.75%; 2025 3.5%; 2026 5.97%/4.75%.
(d) Non-competes [M]: 18% covered (15–21%), earn 4% less (association) (e61 Oct 2024); job mobility 7.2% yr to Feb-2026; draft ban 7 Sep 2026 to $190,100, from 2027 earliest.
(e) Labour supply [M slack/L migration]: NOM 538k→306k; underemployment 6.3% Sep-2026; pre-2020 slack explained most RBA wage-forecast misses (RBA 2017); no primary estimate of migration's wage effect.
(f) Public-sector caps [H]: NSW repealed 2.5% cap Nov-2023; VIC 1.5%→3%; public WPI 3.4% > private 3.1% for six quarters.
(g) Care economy [H]: government funds both sides: aged care +15% from Jul-2023 (18–28.5% total for direct care by Oct-2025); childcare +10% Dec-2024 + 5% Dec-2025 ($3.6b).
(h) Catch-up [H]: real WPI still below pre-pandemic; 2026 CPI 3.9% vs WPI 3.2%.

## 3. Who decides / what sustains it
Hurdle rates: boards/CFOs (career risk, no competitive pressure). Concentration: incumbents; merger law weak until 2026; state regulatory fragmentation (OECD 2026). Tax: revenue needs; 2026 Budget chose property tax changes over cash-flow tax. Wages: FWC, state treasuries (budget repair), Commonwealth (care funding).

## 4. Verdict
Investment drivers by evidential weight: (1) mining unwind; (2) weak MFP/demand and declining competition; (3) risk premium/hurdle rates; (4) tax (modelled); (5) approvals; (6) SME finance; rest low. Wage drivers: (1) productivity; (2) inflation shocks; (3) slack and public-sector caps; (4) mobility/non-competes; (5) bargaining structure. 2025 agenda addresses mergers (mandatory notification from 1 Jan 2026), non-competes, EPBC, small-business tax; ignores company tax base, risk premium, state fragmentation. Within 5 years: data centres (narrow), partial expensing, approvals that work, competition enforcement; even the PC's full tax package adds only ≈2.2% to investment.
