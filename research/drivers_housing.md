# Driver tree: housing supply, prices and rents 2019–26 (agent output, 2026-10-26)
Confidence: [H] high, [M] medium, [L] low. Corrections to premises: zoning ~42% of Sydney detached price is Kendall & Tulip (RBA RDP 2018-03), not Saunders–Tulip; 2021 boom +22.4%; −53% completions/hour ignores size/quality (quality-adjusted −12%).

## 1. Supply
Outcome: completions 219k (2018) → 177k (2024) → 173k (2025); higher-density 62k in 2025, −41% vs 2017 peak. First 18 months of Accord: 263k completed (232k net) vs 287k underlying demand; 1.2m now expected ~Sep 2030 (NHSAC 2026). [H]
(a) Construction productivity [M]: completions/hour −53% 1994-95→2022-23; VA/hour −12% vs economy +49%; houses 193→232 m². Why: fragmentation (top-4 share 12%, avg 1.5 employees), NCC updates adopted despite net-cost RIAs and state divergence ("chill" on prefab), apprenticeships stagnant (commencements −13.7% in 2024-25). Defects shock: Opal (Dec 2018), Mascot (2019); 97% of audited NSW buildings had ≥1 defect [M]; no quantified approvals effect found [L]. Insolvencies: fixed-price contracts vs input inflation peaking 17.3% y/y Jun-2022; first-time construction administrations 2,977 (FY24), 3,596 (FY25), ~3,450 (FY26); NHSAC 3,325 in yr to Mar-2026, 44% NSW [M].
(b) Planning/zoning [H direction, M size]: zoning effect ≈A$489k on a Sydney detached house (2016), growing; NSW PC: planning adds >50% to new apartment costs, blanket heritage; Grattan: 3 storeys everywhere (6 near transport) → prices/rents −7% after 5 yrs, −12% after 10; approval times NSW ~84–115 days, VIC ~134–145 vs 60-day statutory [L].
(c) Taxes/charges [M]: CIE/HIA Mar-2025: 49% (~$576k) of a $1.18m Sydney greenfield package is taxes/charges/regulatory cost (43% Melbourne; Sydney +38%/+$160k since 2019). Caveat: industry-commissioned, counts GST and supply-chain income tax; much incidence falls on land values.
(d) Finance/feasibility [H]: Sydney mid-rise apartment cost $666k (2018) → $905k (2023) → $1.05m (2025); feasible mainly east of Parramatta; APRA (Feb 2025) clarified 100% presales not required; 2026 rate rises worsen feasibility; state foreign-investor surcharges deter BTR.
(e) Labour/materials [H]: ~140k extra workers needed for Accord; apartment cost/m² +18% (2023-24), +10.2% (2024-25); migrant trades "a small proportion" of construction workforce.
(f) Land [M]: greenfield lot sales near lows; median lot record $391k (Sep-q 2025).
(g) Government building [H]: HAFF 1,432 of 40,000 homes by May 2026; public housing stock −1,143 (2024-25), community −392; Victoria 56.3k completions vs 80k target.

## 2. Demand
(a) Population [H]: NOM 518k (2022-23 initial est.), ¾ temporary incl. 283k students; peak 556k → 311k (yr to Sep-2025).
(b) Rates [H]: cash rate 0.10% from Nov-2020; TFF $188bn (~6% of credit); Saunders & Tulip: sustained 1pt real-rate cut → prices ≈+28% long run; 2021 +22.4%; 2025 cuts drove that year's rise.
(c) Tax settings [M]: NG + CGT discount raise prices ~1–2% (Grattan Dec-2025 ~1%): level effect, little of the 2019–26 change.
(d) FHB subsidies [M]: Treasury +0.5% over 6 yrs for expanded 5% scheme; states' stamp-duty concessions >$1.6bn (2023-24); NHSAC: push prices up for FHBs as a group.
(e) Household size [H]: 2.55 → 2.48 (late 2020→Aug 2022) ≈ +120k households, much of the rental demand; partly reversed since (adults/household 2.08→2.10).
(f) Investors [H]: ~40% of new lending (Sep-q 2025); investor loans +22.2% (2024), +11.7% (2025).
(g) Foreign buyers [M]: NAB Q1 2025: 6.9% of new, 3.1% of established sales (averages 9%/5%); established purchases banned since Apr 2025.
(h) Family wealth [L]: 17% of FHBs had family deposit help (11% in 2022).
Result: dwelling value-to-income record 8.2 (Sep 2025) vs 6.8 20-yr average.

## 3. Rents [H]
Vacancy trough 1.4% (Mar-2024) → 2.0% (Dec-2025) vs 2.4% 15-yr avg; rents +40.6% in 5 yrs to Jun-2026; rent share of median income record 33.1%. RBA: demand relative to stock drives rents, little direct cash-rate pass-through; rents rose in 2021 before migration resumed (household-size effect). Students [L, contested]: interested-party studies find weak link; inner-city pressure reported. Short-term rentals [M]: local (Byron ~18% of stock; 5.2% across 11 coastal areas; Waverley 5.6%). Rent assistance capture by landlords: no credible estimate [L].

## 4. Incentives sustaining each driver
States: stamp duty >1/5 of revenue → deters land-tax switch; buyer concessions add demand. Councils/NIMBY: incumbents capture amenity, newcomers bear costs → blanket heritage; NSW imposed determination targets [M]. Commonwealth: controls migration, rates (via RBA), tax, but no planning power; pays for outcomes (Accord incentives, HAFF), slow [H]. Industry: risk sits on small, thinly capitalised builders on fixed-price contracts [M].

## 5. Verdict (agent synthesis, L–M)
| Driver | Share of price rise | Share of rent rise | Reversible within |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interest rates & credit (2020–21, 2025) | 30–40% | 5–10% | 1 yr |
| Inelastic supply: zoning/planning (the multiplier) | 20–30% | 20–30% | 5–10 yrs |
| Population surge + household-formation swing | 15–25% | 35–50% | 1 yr (visas) |
| Cost/productivity/insolvency shock (apartments) | 10–15% | 10–20% | 5 yrs costs, 10 yrs productivity |
| Taxes/charges on new housing | 5–10% | 5% | 5–10 yrs |
| NG/CGT, FHB subsidies, foreign buyers, STR | <5% each | <5% each | 1 yr, small effect |
Demand shocks were big but temporary; the lasting problem is supply that cannot respond (completions no higher than 2018 despite record prices).
Gaps: FY22–23 insolvencies; quantified Opal/Mascot effect; prefab share; rent-assistance pass-through.
