# Driver tree: NDIS integrity and cost growth (agent output, 2026-10-26)
Tags: [O] official, [C] claimed/secondary. Main finding: provable fraud ≈1–6% of growth; most growth is scheme design (uncapped, Commonwealth bears marginal cost, plan inflation, prices at caps, eligibility drift). Controls were weak for a decade.

## 1. Fraud and leakage
- NDIA Sep-2023 [O]: 6–10% of outlays fraud/non-compliance/error = $2.0–3.5b (2022-23), forecast $3.6–6.0b by 2027-28; 10% "medium confidence" (ANAO S1). ACIC chief Aug-2022 [C]: 15–20%. No ministerial "$2b" quote found.
- Latest [O]: "broad integrity leakage" 8.2–8.3% of ≈$45b ≈ $3.7b (2024-25): 1.3% likely serious offending; 4.5% unclear (payment-error testing); 1.9% non-compliance/sharp practice; 0.5% other. NDIA: "no statistically valid way" to isolate fraud (JSC Integrity report Jul-2026 S2).
- Fraud Fusion Taskforce (since Nov-2022) [O]: 25 prosecutions to Mar-2026 (17 jail); restitution $3.5m (≈0.1% of one year's leakage); 411 investigations (Apr-2026) / 525+ matters, 108 operations (Jun-2026) / "600+" (NDIA evidence) — counts inconsistent; 77 warrants in 2025 vs 30 in 2018–21; >2,500 providers disrupted (claimed $1b prior 12 months, not all fraud).
- Cases 2026 [C, alleged]: Darwin NDIA employee's company $28m claimed/$18m suspicious; Adelaide $5m plot; QLD $5.02m restrained.
- ANAO 25 Jun 2025 [O]: NDIA "partly effective"; pre-2024 prevention controls internally "catastrophically weak"; pre-payment review covered 0.4% of dollars yet >50% of reviewed value cancelled; 2023-24 payment error rate 5.0% reported as 4.19%.
- Butler 22 Apr 2026 [C]: NDIA has "no visibility of evidence" for 90% of claims (~600k/day).
- Implied: serious fraud ≈1.3–3% of spend (≈$0.7–1.5b of 2025-26); total leakage ≈8%.

## 2. Design drivers [O]
- Participants 534,655 (Jun-2022) → 782,013 (Jun-2026), +46%. Participation peaks 11% at age 6 (15% boys). Autism 45% of participants (350,651; from 214,880 Jun-2023 = ≈79% of net growth since). Autism/developmental delay = 78% of 2024-25 entrants; 66% of 2025-26 entrants under 15.
- Plan inflation 9.7% (Jun-q 2025) → 4.7% (Jun-q 2026). Real per-participant growth above prices/mix: 5.6%, 7.4%, 4.8%, 3.9% p.a. (2021-22→2024-25). Unscheduled reassessment raises plan value +20% on average. Utilisation 75.6%.
- Prices at caps: 72% of therapy sessions at/near cap (2021-22, NDIS Review); >90% of therapy and support-coordination claims within 5% of cap (e61 Aug-2025 [C]). No scheme-wide "wedding tax" quantification.
- Unregistered providers: 92% of providers paid via plan managers, 41% of $8.4b quarterly plan-managed claims. Plan-manager fees $166m/quarter; ≈90% of small plan managers show fraud/non-compliance indicators.
- SIL: 36,874 participants (4.7%) cost $16.6b, avg $451,800, +9% p.a.
- Exits: 14,123 non-death exits in 2024-25 = 2.0%.

## 3. Original design vs reality [O]
- PC 2011: 411,250 participants, $12.8b supports. PC 2017: 476,079 and $21.8b (of +$9.0b, $6.4b wages). 2017 estimate for 2025-26: $34.8b vs $51.2b actual.
- NDIS Review 2023: 101k more participants and $8b more than 2017 projection in 2022-23; heading for $92b by 2032-33.
- States: contributions fixed +4% p.a., Commonwealth pays everything above → Cth share 59% (2021-22) → 78% (2032-33).
- "Oasis in the desert": only 4% of disability funding outside the NDIS.

## 4. 2026 reforms [O]
- Act passed 18 Aug 2026: mandatory registration for high-risk providers (target 90% of payments to registered by 2030), provider enrolment, more claim evidence, kickback ban, pricing moved to Minister, $821.2m integrity.
- $37.8b savings by measure: $13.2b cuts to social/community participation and daily-activity budgets; $9.3b functional eligibility test; $3.9b framework planning; $3.1b reassessment rules/ending rollovers; integrity + pricing only $3.7b (≈10%; registration $0.2b).
- Review recommendations not followed/delayed: foundational supports from Oct-2026 but not operational; pricing meant for IHACPA, given to Minister instead.
- Credibility: Budget assumes ≈2% p.a. growth vs 10.5% observed; Actuary baseline already beaten (+$13.9b/5yr); CBA "very unlikely in full"; Grattan: front-loaded, blunt; Parliamentary Library: eligibility may not cut numbers as expected; no public Actuary view.

## 5. International [O]
OECD incapacity spending 2021 (% GDP): Australia 3.13, OECD avg 2.04, Denmark 4.74, Norway 4.04, Sweden 3.25, UK 1.69, NZ 2.59. In-kind services (mostly NDIS): Australia 1.43% (2022) vs OECD 0.49%. OECD: total ≈ average but rising faster than peers; prices cluster at caps. Caveats: series break 2018–19; Canada provincial missing; definitions differ. Australia uniquely: uncapped, individualised, non-means-tested plans.

## 6. Verdict: decomposing $23.3b growth ($27.9b→$51.2b), agent estimate
Average participants +≈52% = +$16.1b; mix (cheaper child plans) −$6.1b → net "more people" $10.0b.

≈47–64% genuine need/legitimate wages; ≈32–54% design failure, over-servicing or fraud. "Responsible"? On controls, no. The overspend was mostly designed in: uncapped demand, states shielded by fixed 4%, no services outside the scheme. Fraud real but small.
