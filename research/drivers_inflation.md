# Driver tree: what drove Australian inflation 2021–23 and 2025–26 (agent output, 2026-10-26)

## 1. Decomposing the 2021–23 surge (peak 7.8% Dec-q 2022)
- RBA (Bulletin Jun 2023, three methods, year to Mar-2023): Shapiro VAR → supply ≈3½pp, demand ≈2½pp, ≈1pp unclassified; Phillips-curve residual → ½–⅔ supply; DSGE → supply ≈¾ of deviation from 2.5%, peaking ≈3½pp Dec-q 2022; without supply shocks underlying inflation would still have been 3.1–3.5%. Caveat (RBA RDP 2024-05): sign restrictions can't cleanly separate supply/demand; treat shares as ±15pp.
- Treasury (FOI, Apr 2023): pass-through of labour costs + import prices (ex fuel/freight) ≈60% of the 7.8%; fuel, freight, construction, energy, rents, fruit & veg the rest; energy only ≈½pp directly.
- Murphy (ANU, Economic Papers 2023; cited by RBA Review): policy stimulus added 3.0pp to 2022 inflation (2.4pp fiscal, 0.6pp monetary looser than normal).
- RBA input-cost decomposition (Aug 2026): 2021 rise tracked import prices; 2022 imports + business returns (mostly mining); from late 2022 labour costs and rents took over.
- Largest CPI contributors 2022: new dwellings +17.8%, domestic travel +19.8%, fuel +13.2%.

## 2. Why demand rose
- Fiscal over-compensation (Murphy): COVID measures $429b (JobKeeper $90b, cash-flow boost $35b, JobSeeker supplements $29b, stage-2 tax cut brought forward $26b). Private real income would have been $101b lower in a no-COVID world; instead ended $110b higher → ≈$2 paid per $1 lost. Consumption $119b below normal → forced saving.
- Saving buffer ≈$260b above trend (RBA 2022) → "close to $300b", ≈20% of disposable income (Feb 2023).
- Monetary: cash rate 0.1% from Nov-2020; "not until 2024" guidance Feb–Nov 2021; 3-yr yield target dropped Nov-2021; bond purchases $281b (Nov-2020–Feb-2022); TFF $188b; first hike May-2022.
- Housing: dwelling values +22.2% yr to Nov-2021 (fastest since 1989); RBA long-run wealth effect ≈3c per $1.
- Labour market: unemployment ≈3½% late 2022, vacancies ≈ unemployed.
- Migration: NOM −85k (2020-21), +204k (2021-22) → no role in the surge; +518k in 2022-23 fed 2023 rents, not the 2022 peak.

## 3. Sector by sector
- New dwellings +20.7% yr to Sep-q 2022 (+17.7% ex HomeBuilder/grant unwind): labour/material shortages + demand pulled forward by grants.
- Energy: wholesale gas ≈$6→$11/GJ, spike ≈$40/GJ May-2022 (coal outages, weather, global prices); electricity +15½% yr to Mar-q 2023; Dec-2022 coal cap modelled to cut 2023-24 rise by 13pp.
- Rents +7.6% yr to Sep-q 2023; CRA increase took 0.3pp off; vacancies near record lows; household size rising again.
- Insurance +16.2% in 2023 (fastest since 2001); reinsurance cause not primary-sourced.
- Food ≈9.6% Sep-2022 (fruit & veg, weather).
- Administered: childcare −13.2% Sep-q 2023 (subsidy); electricity −17.3% Sep-q 2024 (rebates; +0.7% without).

## 4. Profits
- RBA (May 2023): non-mining profit share little changed; large firms' margins unchanged from 2019. Treasury: non-mining implied margins +0.4pp Dec-2019→Dec-2022 while CPI +12.6%. Australia Institute (Feb 2023): 69% of above-target inflation from profits — rests on GDP-deflator accounting dominated by mining exports; mining supplies <2% of household consumption inputs. ACCC supermarkets (Mar 2025): EBIT margins rose but most price rises reflected costs. RBA firm-level: incomplete pass-through, lower in less competitive industries. RBA May 2026: margins "only a modest impact". Verdict: non-mining margins not an independent driver; edges in energy/supermarkets.

## 5. Policy errors
- RBA Review (Apr 2023): "initially slow" in 2022, over-focused on wages, models with little supply side; Oct-2020 shift to acting on actual not forecast inflation "unwise"; forward guidance damaged credibility; bond purchases boosted nominal GDP ≈$25b vs $35–58b losses; TFF cost ≈$8b; cumulative stimulus "contributed to the overshoot".
- IMF (Oct 2024): 2024-25 budget positive fiscal impulse; states "more expansionary than expected". OECD 2023: save commodity windfalls.
- Rebates shifted timing: ≈−½pp headline in 2024-25 (Treasury), ≈+0.5pp on expiry Dec-q 2025.
- Verdict: stimulus too large 2020–21 and too long in 2021; RBA late by 2–3 quarters; fiscal/monetary mildly at cross-purposes 2023–25.

Decomposing +1.8pp headline: ≈0.9pp underlying (capacity, market services 3.9%, new dwellings 5.3% as discounts removed + conflict costs, health insurance); ≈0.5pp electricity rebate expiry (utilities +15.2%); rest fuel/Middle East conflict (fuel +0.8pp in Mar-2026 alone; excise relief −0.5pp from April, unwound in Aug when fuel +14.8% m/m). Unit labour costs 5.0–5.4% through 2025 with productivity ≈0, easing to 3.3%; RBA trend productivity cut to 0.7%. Rents easing to 3.6%. AUD 5–7% above RBA forecast (mildly disinflationary). Tariffs "limited". RBA: capacity "unlikely to explain the majority" of H2-2025 rise; housing/durables demand + volatiles explain most; AI-driven tech prices added Sep-2026. Cash rate 3.60% start-2026 → 4.35% (Feb/Mar/May) → 4.60% (Sep). Oliver (AMP): public demand record ≈28% of GDP; RBA does not cite fiscal policy.

## 7. Verdict (agent)
2021–23 (≈5pp above target): ≈55% supply (goods, shipping, fuel, coal/gas outages, floods, materials); ≈40% domestic demand, mostly fiscal over-compensation saved then spent, ≈⅕ of that monetary; ≈5% non-mining margins; housing/construction straddles both. 2025–26: ≈half genuine excess demand over zero-productivity supply (services, construction, 2025 easing, arguably public demand), ≈half one-offs (rebate unwind, fuel). Levers that work now: tight monetary policy on the capacity gap; fiscal restraint (slower public demand, no new rebates); supply measures (construction workforce/approvals, energy, productivity). Levers that don't: profit-focused policy.
Unconfirmed/corrected: 2021 prices +22.2% (not 25%); early super release $35b by Nov-2020; JobKeeper $89–90b; no primary e61 profits finding; no reinsurance source. Sep-q 2026 CPI due 28 Oct 2026.
