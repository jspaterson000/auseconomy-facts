# Driver tree: why NOM surged 2022–24, decisions, impacts (agent output, 2026-10-26)
"calc" = agent arithmetic; "judgement" = no official source.

## 1. Decomposition
- The miss: Oct-2022 Budget assumed 235k for 2022-23 and 2023-24 "in line with pre-pandemic trends" while noting strong visa issuance and slow departures; Mar-2022 Budget had 180k.
- Outcomes: 538k (2022-23: 738k arrivals, 200k departures); 429k (2023-24: 661k, 232k).
- vs 2014-19 average (515k arrivals, 288k departures, 227k NOM): 2022-23 excess ≈311k = 72% extra arrivals (+223k), 28% missing departures (−88k) (calc).
- By visa 2022-23 (net, calc): students +262k (pre-COVID peak ≈107k); visitors +90k (converting onshore); WHM +71k; temporary skilled +44k; permanent +60k (historical ≈70k); NZ +26k (vs 8k); Australian citizens −30k. 2023-24: students +171k, permanent +72k, WHM +68k, visitors +68k, NZ +37k.
- Bridging visa stock: 180k (Jun-2019), 177k (Jun-2023), 413k (Jun-2026), 446k (Sep-2026 ex BVE).
- Catch-up test: through 2021-22 NOM was 449k below the pre-COVID MYEFO path; by 2023-24 cumulative since 2018-19 only 85k (5.7%) above → most of the surge refilled the pandemic hole.
- Treasury/CfP blamed: pent-up students (incl. existing visa holders), China's Jan-2023 withdrawal of recognition for online degrees, strong labour market; record-low departures (625k onshore temporary grants in 2022-23, record graduate-visa transitions, AAT queues); Pandemic Event (408) holders staying.

## 3. Was it necessary?
- Shortages real: vacancies 480.1k May-2022 (+111% vs Feb-2020): health 68.9k, accommodation/food 51.9k, professional 42.9k, retail 40.3k, construction 39.9k.
- Inflow didn't target them: students 23.4% in accommodation/food (vs 6.6% of all workers); permanent increase mostly relabelled onshore people.
- Universities: international fees $10.2bn (2023) ≈25% of $40.0bn sector revenue; 15–>40% at major unis; education exports ≈$50bn (2023-24).
- Treasury: TSS visas most positive fiscally; students/WHM/graduates "small positive".
- Alternatives available by early 2023: keep 40-hr cap; skip post-study extension; ban visitor→student onshore switching (done Jul-2024); prioritise processing (done Dec-2023); act on Parkinson/Nixon (Mar-2023) when they reported.

## 4. Impacts
- Rents: CPI rents +7.8% yr to Mar-q 2024 (fastest since 2009); vacancy 1.6% (2023); RBA: +100k students ≈ +0.5% rents → ≈4–5% for a ≈500k excess (calc); most of the advertised-rent rise came before borders reopened (household-size effect).
- Cities: Sydney 157k + Melbourne 147k ≈58% of 2022-23 NOM.
- Housing: completions 172k (2023), 177k (2024); 2024 net supply 155k vs underlying demand 223k (−68k); 241k new households in 2023-24.
- Labour: students in hospitality/retail/health; healthcare got 17,061 permanent places 2022-23; no sector wage study found. Services: no causal evidence on hospital waits/schools.
- GDP per capita fell 7 straight quarters to Sep-q 2024; ABS later found population overstated ≈80k.
- Fiscal: students spend ≈ residents ex fees (40% of their spending); 2026-27 points-test + WHM reforms reduce receipts $2.1bn.
- Exploitation: Nixon: ≈75% of students use agents; unis pay ≈$250m/yr to agents; protection decisions avg 841 days; chained applications → ≈decade stays. Parkinson: 1.8m temporary migrants with work rights ≈13% of labour market. e61: "visa hoppers" 2.5%→>25% of graduate cohorts. Refused protection applicants onshore 55k→103k; student refusal appeals 11k→46k, median 65 weeks.

## 5. What changed; is 245k→225k credible?
Measures: 48-hr cap; 408 closed; higher savings/English; Genuine Student test; onshore switching bans; shorter graduate visas, age limit 35; graduate visa fee doubled 1 Mar 2026. Caps bill failed Senate Nov-2024; planning level steers offshore processing only. Offshore student lodgements −106k to 257k (2024-25). Budget path: 295k, 245k, 225k. Latest: 292.1k yr to Mar-2026, quarterly rebound to 101k. Stocks 30 Sep 2026: bridging 446k, graduate 278k, students 551k, WHM 218k. Departures the weak point: 2025 Statement assumed 305k in 2025-26; actual 265k. Agent call: 2026-27 NOM 250–290k (judgement).

## 6. Verdict (share of ≈497k excess over 2×235k, judgement)
(a) Mechanical reopening catch-up 55–65% (hole nearly refilled; 85k overshoot; young cohorts, few departures). (b) Deliberate policy for shortages 10–15% (processing surge, work hours; 195k program little). (c) Unintended settings 15–25% (408 retention, onshore churn, bridging/appeal queues, visitor→student). (d) Institutional incentives 5–15% (unis, private VET, agents). Lasting legacy = inflated onshore stocks, not the 2022-23 peak.
