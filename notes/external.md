# External sector and energy (BOP and CPI to 2026-Q2, merchandise trade to Aug 2026)

**Current account.** The 2026-Q2 deficit is 3.7% of GDP (seasonally adjusted), about the 1980-2025 average (-3.6%) and well short of the 2007 trough (-7.3%). But it has swung 6.9 points since the 2021-Q3 surplus (+3.2%). The goods and services balance has just turned negative (-0.7% of GDP; imports 24.2%, exports 23.5%). Terms of trade (94.8) are 18% below the 2022-Q2 peak. Net primary income (-2.9%) is at its long-run norm, so the widening is mostly a trade-price story.

**China and iron ore.** In 2025-26 China took 35.1% of merchandise exports ($187bn), down from 39.4% in 2019-20. Iron ore is 22.3% of goods exports and 85.5% of it goes to China (68% in 2010-11), so iron ore to China alone is about 19% of goods exports. Coal (12.6%) and LNG (10.4%) are losing share; gold has surged to 13.1% (6.4% in 2019-20), and 18% of it now goes to China. Education-related travel is $53.6bn (2024-25, latest), 39.9% of services exports, with China the source of 25.5% of it.

**Energy vs CPI.** From 2019-Q4 to 2026-Q2, electricity CPI is up 28.0%, gas 44.2%, headline CPI 26.8%. Gas clearly outpaced CPI. Electricity is only just ahead, and measured from end-2018 it is behind (+23.5% vs +29%), so the answer depends on the base. The energy weight in the CPI fell from 3.5% (2018) to 2.8% (not a full household budget share).

**Bill relief.** It is visible. The ABS electricity index is net of rebates: it fell 17% in 2024-Q3 and electricity inflation reached -25% in 2024-Q4. At a weight near 2.4% that took roughly 0.6 points off headline CPI (my estimate, not an ABS figure). The rebates' unwinding put electricity inflation at 22% to 32% across the last four quarters, adding a similar amount.
